## Inspiration

**Keep the lesson moving. Calm the idle blink.**

A teacher has a finished screen recording, but the editable project is gone. The command is already written; a caret keeps blinking through the explanation. Freezing the whole picture would also freeze the progress readout. Blurring the teaching area could hide what students need to read.

Frame Review makes a smaller promise: let an editor choose one observed detail to hold, then check the actual export against the teaching content they protected. The original upload stays recoverable. The teacher decides whether the blink is useful.

## What it does

Upload a supported MP4/MOV/WebM lesson, choose a short excerpt, inspect both actual source states, name the teaching areas that must stay unchanged, and check the exact plan. Only explicit approval generates a comparison. The app holds an observed source state inside one small box and interval; it invents no replacement picture.

**New: inspect what keeps moving.** Protected code, labels and progress have an ordered RGB proof across all 360 prepared-source and encoded-edit frames. Static PNG pairs show hold entry, the largest measured teaching change, and hold end. The same number of changes in the wrong order cannot pass. Corrupted proof receipts and PNGs are withheld.

## The cut points were a real failure

Earlier fixed holds could remove the interior blink while making the return worse. The current source-matched policy trims within the detected interval, chooses an actual reference from the same appearance class, and keeps the clip's first and last frames unchanged. It never extends the detected window.

The actual encoded result must have no worse entry, return or loop differences in selected/influence RGB mean absolute, RMS and maximum error, plus the frozen selected-mean diagnostic. Native samples outside the box, every outside-interval frame, all protected RGB, RGB outside influence, timestamps and prepared audio/absence must remain exact. A worsening cut or newly timed activity event withholds the edit.

The earlier **same-72-second / six-source comparison** remains versioned: source **90**, one untuned local 5×5/sigma2 blur **91**, prior longer hold **54**, source-matched hold **56**. The matched hold removes 34 changes (37.8%) with no new events; the prior hold introduces events in five cases. The shorter matched holds total 17.6 seconds versus 24 before. Full traces and continuous measurements are included. This is an engineering reference, not a claim of superiority over professional editors.

## How we built it

OpenCV 5 provides spatial and temporal video analysis, region proposals and image evidence. A separately versioned stationary two-state detector proposes small observed-state holds. An unchanged rapid-decoration retimer remains a separate option. Deterministic checks bind the source, prediction, explicit teaching areas, approved recipe and output.

Pinned FFmpeg prepares the bounded excerpt, muxes the edit and supports independent native-YUV, decoded-RGB, timing and audio checks. Python runs one constrained worker. The browser serves static inspection rather than autoplay. Exact original/prepared downloads, recipe, report and decision support recovery and reproduction.

AWS ECR stores the immutable image, ECS/Fargate runs the existing one-task public app, and CloudWatch captures runtime logs. No application task role was added. The deployed manifest binds **108 exact runtime files**. The full Linux gate passes **14 stages, 137 backend tests and 91 studio tests** (one platform skip), including actual encoding and refusal of the old bad Jupyter boundary. Actual AWS original-upload browser/download/playback evidence is separate from local qualification and recorded in the report.

## Impact and honest limits

The impact hypothesis is a teacher or editor rescuing a short explanation without re-recording a lesson. What we have measured is a bounded change with exact declared teaching samples, preserved prepared narration or silence, checked cut points and recoverable sources. We have not measured creator preference, minutes saved, attention, comprehension, accessibility, safety or adoption. A consenting creator preference and timed-task study is the next useful evidence.

Supported SDR originals are limited to 64 MiB and prepared 1–12 second excerpts. Preparation resizes/re-encodes picture and optional mono AAC once and can reduce legibility; exact edit checks reference the prepared clip, not an unchanged original. Protection uses fixed rectangles, with no full-lesson splice-back or moving semantic tracking. Jobs expire after 24 hours and can disappear sooner on restart. Download results you want to keep.
